# Imported Research: H1/H2 and L1/L2

Source: read-only review of the user's `Codex Trading` repository, especially its Brooks pattern inventory, pullback-leg research, practice plan, and KLAC High-1/High-2 case studies.

This document is an imported research summary. It does not promote any rule to production and does not modify the source repository.

## Main conclusion

H1/H2 and L1/L2 should not be treated as isolated labels or automatic setups. They are descriptive tags inside a larger structure:

1. An established directional impulse or a directional leg inside a trading range.
2. A meaningful countertrend correction.
3. One or more completed countertrend legs.
4. A test of a previously known structural support/resistance zone.
5. A signal and confirmation in the original direction.
6. Enough space to the first independent obstacle.
7. A defined structural stop and invalidation condition.

Without these conditions, an H1/H2 or L1/L2 label is only descriptive and should not authorize a trade.

## H1/H2 and L1/L2 interpretation

- H1: the first upward attempt during a pullback in an upward context.
- H2: the second upward attempt after the first attempt fails or the pullback develops another meaningful leg.
- L1: the first downward attempt during a pullback in a downward context.
- L2: the second downward attempt after the first attempt fails or the pullback develops another meaningful leg.
- H3/L3: usually descriptive of a third attempt, triangle, wedge, or three-push correction; not automatically a higher-quality setup.
- A double bottom can be a visual H2 bull flag; a double top can be a visual L2 bear flag. The labels are nested and context-dependent.

The countertrend legs must be meaningful. Four or more correction legs should not be forced into H4/L4; they are better recorded as complex correction or range noise.

## Location is a hard gate

The imported research gives structural location priority over indicator contact:

- The correction should test a previously established major or intermediate support/resistance zone.
- The zone must have a visible structural anchor, such as repeated prior highs/lows, a range boundary, a breakout/retest area, or a gap edge.
- The zone must be known before the reaction; a level discovered only after the reaction cannot be used to explain it.
- The correction should touch and hold the zone. A decisive close through the zone is a failed location test.
- EMA20/EMA50/EMA200 and VWAP may provide context, but an EMA touch alone cannot create an H/L setup or a valid location.

## Confirmation and causality

The research separates pattern recognition from executable confirmation:

- A signal bar is not the same as an entry.
- For a stop-entry research model, the next bar must break the signal bar's relevant extreme in the intended direction.
- The entry, stop, and invalidation must be based only on information available before the entry.
- The latest pivot cannot be used before its required right-side confirmation is complete.
- A large shock bar, gap, or climax should trigger a cooling-off/observation state rather than being counted as an ordinary H/L signal.
- A gap through the entry or stop can invalidate the assumed 1R execution geometry.

## Strong impulse and correction depth

The source research distinguishes a strict strong impulse from a merely large net move. A strict continuation impulse is more credible when it has directional closes, limited overlap, several bars of continuity, and possibly a gap. A large move with heavy overlap should not automatically receive the same H1 priority.

The imported KLAC calibration uses these as research buckets, not permanent rules:

- Shallow correction after a strict strong impulse: H1 can remain an observation branch.
- Deep correction, weak/overlapping impulse, or poor first signal: defer priority to H2.
- The source study used a 50% correction boundary and an EMA50 proximity bucket for comparison, but explicitly did not promote them to Brooks universal rules or production gates.

## Support-based stop logic

The imported High-2 case study shows why a stop just below the latest signal bar can be too tight. If several prior lows form a support band, the stop may still sit inside that band and be vulnerable to a normal retest.

For research, compare at least:

- local stop below the signal structure;
- structural stop below the full repeated-support zone;
- wider backup stop only when the trade plan explicitly allows the larger structural risk.

The stop should be chosen before entry and evaluated against the first obstacle and measured-move target. A favorable historical R/R calculation does not prove a tradable edge.

## What to carry into PA Research

The most useful reusable template is:

| Field | Required question |
| --- | --- |
| Context | Trend, range leg, or transition? |
| Impulse | Is the prior leg directional and sufficiently clean? |
| Correction | How many meaningful countertrend legs? |
| Location | Which pre-existing structural zone is being tested? |
| Attempt | H1/H2 or L1/L2, and why? |
| Signal | What is the signal bar and what makes it valid? |
| Confirmation | What price action confirms the entry? |
| Space | Where is the first independent obstacle? |
| Stop | Local or structural, and why? |
| Invalidation | What proves the hypothesis wrong? |
| Outcome | Follow-through, failure, range, or reversal? |

## Boundaries

- The source repository's code, thresholds, scores, production gates, and execution contracts are not imported as ready-to-trade rules.
- The source study's sample counts are research evidence, not win rates.
- A chart can contain nested H/L labels, wedges, channels, ABC legs, and measured moves simultaneously; naming more patterns does not create more probability.
- The next PA Research step is manual chart adjudication of representative long H1/H2 examples, followed by the symmetric short L1/L2 cases.
