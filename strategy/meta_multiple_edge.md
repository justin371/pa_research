# META — Multiple Edge Trading Area

## Definition

META means **Multiple Edge Trading Area**.

It is a price area where several independent trading advantages converge. META is a location framework, not an entry signal by itself.

The purpose of META is to identify places where the probability of a meaningful reaction is higher than usual.

## Common Edges

A META may include:

- Higher-timeframe support or resistance
- Prior breakout or gap area
- Three-push wedge
- Measured Move completion
- Trend-channel boundary
- Climax or exhaustion behavior
- Failed breakout
- Strong reversal bar
- Volume and price confirmation
- Clear space to the first target

The more independent edges converge, the stronger the location may be.

## Core Principle

A single pattern is rarely enough.

A higher-quality setup combines:

1. A meaningful location
2. A recognizable price-action pattern
3. Evidence of weakening or rejection
4. A clear entry trigger
5. A defined structural stop
6. Sufficient space to the first target

## Expected Reaction

A META increases the probability of a reaction, but it does not guarantee a full trend reversal.

The first expectation should usually be:

- A pause
- A bounce
- A pullback
- A test of the prior level

A larger reversal requires follow-through and a break of the relevant lower high or higher low.

## Example: Bullish META

A bullish META may form when:

- Price reaches higher-timeframe support
- A three-push wedge develops downward
- Each push shows weaker selling momentum
- A Measured Move target is completed
- Price forms a strong reversal signal
- The first target has adequate space

This creates a potential long setup, but the trade still requires confirmation.

## Example: Bearish META

A bearish META may form when:

- Price reaches higher-timeframe resistance
- A three-push wedge develops upward
- Each push shows weaker buying momentum
- A Measured Move target is completed
- Price fails to break higher
- A strong bearish reversal signal appears

This creates a potential short setup, but the trade still requires confirmation.

## Entry Logic

Do not enter only because price has reached a META.

A valid trade requires a price-action trigger, such as:

- Break above the signal bar
- Break below the signal bar
- Strong reversal bar with follow-through
- Failed breakout followed by reversal
- Break of the short-term trendline
- Break and successful test of the relevant swing point

The trade should be expressed conditionally:

> If price reaches the META, shows the required trigger, and has sufficient space to the first obstacle, then enter. Otherwise remain flat.

## Stop and Invalidation

The stop should be placed beyond the structural reason for the trade.

Examples:

- Below the third-push low for a bullish setup
- Above the third-push high for a bearish setup
- Beyond the support or resistance area
- Beyond the failed-breakout extreme

The setup is invalid if:

- Price breaks and accepts beyond the structural level
- The third push continues with strong momentum
- The expected reversal has no follow-through
- Price reaches the trigger area repeatedly without progress

## Target Logic

Measured Move projections may be used as target areas.

Targets should be treated as zones, not exact prices.

Near the first target:

- Take partial profits when appropriate
- Watch for rejection or exhaustion
- Reassess the thesis
- Do not assume the trend must reverse

If price reaches the target without reacting, the original thesis may be wrong and a new structure should be evaluated.

## Risk Management

META is a location advantage, not a guarantee of success.

Risk should be reduced when:

- The broader market disagrees with the trade
- The setup is countertrend
- The support or resistance zone is wide
- The first target is too close
- The trigger is weak or ambiguous

Do not increase size simply because several edges appear to overlap.

## Quality Checklist

Before entering, ask:

- What are the independent edges?
- Is the location meaningful?
- Is the pattern clearly defined?
- Is momentum weakening or strengthening?
- What is the exact trigger?
- Where is structural invalidation?
- What is the first obstacle?
- Is there enough space for a reasonable reward-to-risk?
- What would prove the idea wrong?

## No-Trade Conditions

Remain flat when:

- The location is unclear
- The pattern is subjective
- There is no trigger
- The stop is too wide
- The first target is too close
- Price is breaking strongly through the supposed support or resistance
- The setup depends on hindsight-only information

## Summary

META is the convergence of multiple independent edges at a meaningful price location.

It improves trade selection by answering:

> Where is a reaction more likely?

It does not answer by itself:

> When should I enter?

The entry still requires a clear price-action trigger, a structural stop, defined targets, and evidence that the expected reaction is beginning.
