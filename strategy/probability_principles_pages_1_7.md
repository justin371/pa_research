## How to use the numbers

- Treat each percentage as conditional on the stated market context.
- The environment comes first: trend, trading range, gap, climax, and location relative to support or resistance.
- A probability does not replace a signal bar, order trigger, stop placement, or reward-to-risk check.
- Do not convert these figures directly into a backtest win rate without defining the setup and sampling rules.

## 90% principles

- About 90% of bars occur within a trading range context; even strong trends contain trading-range behavior.
- On a strong bull-trend day with many bars above the moving average, the market still has a very high likelihood of trading below the EMA at least briefly.
- Regardless of trend strength, a day usually reaches the EMA at some point.
- A breakout phase is commonly followed by a pullback and then a channel, trading range, or climax; a bull breakout reversing immediately is the less common outcome.
- A quality swing opportunity during the day often begins with a clear signal bar, sufficient movement, at least two legs, and enough potential reward relative to risk.

## 80% principles

- In a trend, roughly 80% of reversal attempts fail; therefore, the first presumption is trend continuation until the reversal has adequate evidence.
- In a trading range, roughly 80% of breakout attempts fail; therefore, failed-breakout and second-entry ideas deserve priority near range edges.
- After a large gap down, a pullback during the first couple of hours is common.
- After an extended move far below the EMA, a pullback to the EMA often leads to a test of the prior low, but a much larger continuation move is less common.

## 70%–75% principles

- An unusually large or surprising bar often produces a measured move, especially when there is at least some follow-through.
- Two or more consecutive large bull bars closing near their highs with little overlap often produce at least a measured move up; the bearish equivalent applies in a bear trend.
- A bull-triangle breakout commonly pulls back toward the triangle apex.
- A large outside-down day usually closes in its lower portion; a close above the midpoint is less common.
- A large gap down often develops into a downward swing after the opening period and is also more likely to form a trading-range day.
- After a climactic prior day, the following day commonly spends time in a trading range.
- A breakout from a bull channel has a high chance of failing or pulling back within the next several bars; the bear-channel equivalent also applies.

## 60% setup group

These are candidate setups that need explicit definitions and testing before being called high-win strategies:

- H2 pullback to the EMA in a bull trend.
- L2 pullback to the EMA in a bear trend.
- Wedge bull-flag pullback in a bull trend.
- Wedge bear-flag pullback in a bear trend.
- Breakout-pullback entry from a bull flag in a bull trend.
- Breakout-pullback entry from a bear flag in a bear trend.
- H1 pullback in a strong bull spike, provided there is no buy climax.
- L1 pullback in a strong bear spike, provided there is no sell climax.
- Shorting the top of a trading range, especially on a second entry.
- Buying the bottom of a trading range, especially on a second entry.
- Trend reversal after a strong trendline break, a test of the trend extreme, and a good reversal signal bar.
- Strong final-flag reversal.

The third/fourth-push and advanced limit-order examples are recorded as future study items, not current priority setups.

## 50%, 35%, 20%, and 10% reminders

- Strong moves immediately after the open can reverse about half the time; this is context, not an automatic fade signal.
- After a buy climax, follow-through buying the next day is possible but not dependable enough to trade without a clear setup.
- After two large late bear bars, the market can be near the end of the selloff, but it can also make one more small leg; the situation is often too balanced for a high-quality scalp.
- The chance that a gap closes on the same day is materially lower than certainty; do not assume gap closure.
- In a trend, reversal success is the minority case; in a trading range, successful breakout is the minority case.
- A market in breakout mode can move quickly while searching for a new trading range.

## Project interpretation

For PA Research, these principles are used in this order:

1. Classify the market environment.
2. Locate major and minor support/resistance.
3. Apply the relevant conditional probability.
4. Identify the Price Action pattern and second-entry context.
5. Choose the order type, stop, target, and invalidation.
6. Record the case for later testing.

The percentages guide expectations. They do not authorize an entry by themselves.
